Punchlist

Talk through your app. Ship the fixes.

Punchlist records your iPhone screen while you narrate what's wrong. It detects each unique screen, transcribes your words on-device, and pairs them into a clean punch list — then serves it over your own Wi-Fi to Claude or any AI coding tool that speaks MCP. Nothing ever touches the cloud.

Connect your computer

Requires Node.js 20 or later. Your phone and computer must be on the same Wi-Fi network.

  1. Enable sharing on your phone.
    In Punchlist, open the Connect tab and switch on Share over local network. A 6-digit pairing code appears. Sharing stays on across launches until you turn it off.
  2. Add the MCP server.
    For Claude Code, run: 
    claude mcp add punchlist -- npx -y punchlist-mcp
    For Claude Desktop, add this to claude_desktop_config.json: 
    {
  "mcpServers": {
    "punchlist": {
      "command": "npx",
      "args": ["-y", "punchlist-mcp"]
    }
  }
}
  3. Pair once.
    Ask Claude to “pair with my Punchlist device” and give it the 6-digit code from the Connect tab. Pairing pins your phone's TLS certificate — from then on every transfer is encrypted end-to-end on your network.
  4. Ship the fixes.
    Record a session on your phone, then tell Claude to “pull my latest punchlist session.” It gets every screenshot, your synced transcript, and your standing per-app instructions — and marks the session processed when it's done.

How it works

  • On-device transcription. Your narration never leaves the phone as audio.
  • Automatic screen detection. Each unique screen becomes one punch-list entry, paired with the words you said while it was visible.
  • Local network only. Sessions are served over TLS on your Wi-Fi with a certificate your computer pins at pairing. No cloud, no accounts.
  • Per-app organization. Register the apps you're building; every session is tagged, filtered, and delivered with that app's custom processing instructions.
  • Closes the loop. When your AI tool finishes acting on a session, it's marked processed on the phone — even if the phone was asleep at the time.
